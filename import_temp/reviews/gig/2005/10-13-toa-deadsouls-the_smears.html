Dead Souls + The Smears (uk) + Ape Of Death

13/10/05 - The Old Angel (Nottingham)

Bleached Press Presents:

So we are going back to The Old Angel, and as always it's dark, stinky and sticky but this doesn't stop first act Dead Souls from getting really stuck in to their set. As their roaring riffs flow out the speakers and the rough husky vocals of lead guitarist Mike are spat out, it is more than evident that Dead Souls have one aim tonight, to play relentless dirty raw grunge that fucking rocks.
Their sound tonight is really deep and the bass seems very prominent, which really suits the songs and drags the melodies along nicely, also adding more noticeable 'twiddly' bits. The drums and guitar riffs are simple, powerful and move along like a horse drawn carriage flying down a steep cobble street, it clatters and bangs along but it looks like a lot of fun and still has plenty of respectability.
The bassist's backing vocals are a lot softer and more melodic, contrasting well with the raw screaming and growling of Mike's more traditional grunge lead vocal. Dead Souls really know how to write catchy songs, which have plenty of power and feeling and tonight they seem to play their set without even breaking a sweat at times.
The thing that always stands out about this band is that even though they play dark and emotional angry grunge in the Nirvana mould their songs somehow stay upbeat and have a cheerful feel, which rather than making you want to slit your wrists, Dead Souls are more inclined to put a smile on your face.

Next up are 3 piece female punk band, The Smears and from the moment they take to the stage there is an air of confidence surrounding them and a "Fuck You" attitude.
The deep, fast, pounding guitar and bass move brilliantly, changing tempo and style at just the right times to keep the songs simple and to the point. The bass has a lot of power and with a deep roar adds beef as well as movement while the guitar riffs are simple and in your face. This is vintage punk with a real original twist.
The vocals have a traditional 'don't give a fuck' lazy punk attitude and even though the lyrics can't always be heard, it has a really upbeat, blunt and furious punk influence.
There is also good movement from both the bassist and guitarist, giving them plenty of presence and a real attitude that suits their style.
Possibly the most impressive and interesting thing about The Smears though tonight is the drumming. The drum patterns were really varied and with a more metal influence at times, gave The Smears a really interesting sound and added diversity. With the roaming and tight bass, the drummer kept the songs really moving and flowing, giving them a really tight and different feel for a punk band. The Smears have enough originality and attitude to really go places.

Finally to finish the night off, our ears are blown out by the gut-wrenching screams, head-crushing guitar riffs and deafening drumming of Ape Of Death.
With three guitarists who interlock their crazy, fast and mental soloing before unleashing a barrage of grinding riffs down onto you, like a pelting storm of noise, Ape Of Death have more bang than the big-bang itself.
The frontman roams around the room, trying his hardest to scream his lower guts out through his throat and he seems to break microphones with ease.
The songs consistently change and move, with the tight drumming dragging them along and giving the guitars chance to show off and build the noise and power even more. This is hard, heavy, filthy metal and at times borders on Gothic.
With the mix of brutal guitar riffs and growls, screams and grunts from the energetic vocalist, it makes your diaphragm bend to breaking point.
If you like your music loud and angry you'll love it, if not it will probably make you mess your pants. 'Heavy as fuck!'



Review - Pedro (Fly On The Wall Promotions)
